# Outreach Best Practices for Probate Leads

## Core Philosophy

Probate outreach is fundamentally different from other motivated seller marketing. You are contacting people in grief, often overwhelmed by legal and financial complexity they did not ask for. The tone must always be helpful, patient, and dignified. Investors who lead with empathy consistently outperform those who lead with urgency.

## Letter Best Practices

**Personalization is mandatory.** Generic mass-mail letters perform poorly in probate. Address the letter to the personal representative by name (confirmed from court records), reference the specific property address, and mention the decedent by name in a respectful way. This signals you have done your homework and are not a bulk mailer.

**Quality signals credibility.** Use high-quality paper stock (at minimum 24lb), clean professional letterhead, and a real signature. Handwritten envelopes outperform printed labels significantly — even imperfect handwriting reads as personal attention.

**Lead with compassion, not business.** The first sentence of your first letter should acknowledge the family's loss. Do not start with your offer or qualifications. The family needs to feel seen as human beings before they can hear your business pitch.

**Keep it short.** One page maximum. Personal representatives are busy and often stressed. Three short paragraphs is the target: (1) condolences and who you are, (2) what you offer and why it may help, (3) soft call to action with zero pressure.

**Avoid predatory language.** Never reference equity amounts, estate value, or what you think the property is worth. Never use language like "motivated seller" or "distressed property." These signal that you are opportunistic rather than helpful.

## Response Rate Benchmarks

| Touch Type | Typical Response Rate |
|------------|----------------------|
| Initial letter | 1-3% |
| Followup letter | 1-2% |
| Phone call (warm) | 8-15% |
| Door knock | 10-20% |
| Overall sequence conversion | 3-7% of contacted leads |

A-grade leads should produce appointment rates of 10-15% when all 6 touches are executed consistently.

## Phone Call Approach

**Call only after at least one written touch.** Cold-calling a bereaved family feels invasive. A prior letter gives you a reference point — "I sent you a letter a few weeks ago about the property on Oak Street."

**Opening script framework:**
- Acknowledge the situation briefly: "First, I want to say I'm sorry for your loss."
- Establish you are not pressuring them: "I'm not calling to push anything — I just wanted to make sure you received my letter."
- Ask one open question: "Are you and the family thinking about selling the property, or are you still figuring out next steps?"
- Listen, then respond to what they actually say.

**Qualification questions (if they're open):**
1. Are you the personal representative? (Confirm decision-making authority)
2. What is your ideal timeline for resolving the estate?
3. Are you working with a real estate agent yet?
4. What is the property's current condition?
5. Have you received any other offers?

## Door Knock Guidelines

Door knocks are the highest-converting touch but require the most care in probate:

- **Never knock on the property itself** unless you have confirmed the family is staying there temporarily. Knocking at an empty house looks like trespassing.
- Knock at the personal representative's home address, obtained from court records.
- Go on weekday evenings or Saturday mornings — not Sunday.
- Bring a simple one-page leave-behind, not a thick sales packet.
- If no answer, leave a handwritten note, not a flyer.

## Compliance Considerations

- **Do Not Call registry:** Check the National DNC Registry before calling any number. Probate leads are not exempt.
- **State solicitation laws:** Some states restrict written solicitation to recently bereaved families. Research your state's specific rules — California, for example, has specific probate solicitation regulations.
- **CANSPAM and email:** Avoid email outreach to probate leads unless you have a pre-existing relationship. Cold email to estates is legally murky and performs poorly.
- **Attorney contact:** If a probate attorney is listed as the agent for the estate, direct all written contact to the attorney, not the PR directly.
