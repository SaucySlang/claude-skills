# Probate Outreach Letter Templates

Use these templates as the foundation for personalized outreach. Always handwrite or laser-print on quality letterhead. Never use mass-printed labels — handwritten envelopes significantly increase open rates.

---

## Template 1: Initial Contact Letter (Touch 1 — Day 1)

**When to use:** First contact with the estate's personal representative or heir. Tone is empathetic, no pressure, purely informational.

---

[Your Name]
[Your Company]
[Your Address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Phone] | [Email]

[Date]

[Personal Representative Name]
RE: Property at [Property Address]

Dear [First Name],

My name is [Your Name], and I work with families in [County] who have recently inherited property and are considering their options.

I came across the estate of [Decedent Name] in the county records and wanted to reach out personally. First, please accept my sincere condolences for your loss.

If managing or selling the property at [Property Address] is something you're thinking about, I'd welcome the chance to have a no-obligation conversation. I work with a network of local buyers who can close quickly, often in as little as two weeks, which can be helpful when settling an estate.

There is absolutely no pressure or obligation. I simply want you to know that you have options, and I'm here as a resource whenever the time is right for your family.

Warmly,

[Your Signature]
[Your Name]

P.S. If now isn't the right time, please hold on to this letter. I'm happy to answer questions at any point during the process.

## Template 2: Followup Letter (Touch 2 — Day 7)

**When to use:** Second touch if no response to initial letter. Slightly more specific, introduces a soft call-to-action.

---

[Your Name]
[Your Company]
[Your Address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Phone] | [Email]

[Date]

[Personal Representative Name]
RE: Following up — [Property Address]

Dear [First Name],

I sent a letter last week regarding the property at [Property Address]. I wanted to follow up briefly in case my first letter didn't reach you.

Settling an estate involves a lot of moving pieces, and selling real property is often one of the more time-sensitive items. If you're exploring options, I can offer:

- A free, no-obligation cash offer within 48 hours
- Flexible closing timeline to fit your probate schedule
- Purchase in as-is condition — no repairs or cleanout required

I work with sellers throughout [County] and understand that every family's situation is different. There's no pressure here — just a genuine offer to help if and when it makes sense.

Please feel free to call or text me at [Phone] anytime.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Name]

---

## Template 3: Final Offer Letter (Touch 6 — Day 90)

**When to use:** Last contact in the sequence. Positions this as a final outreach, creates mild urgency without being pushy.

---

[Your Name]
[Your Company]
[Your Address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Phone] | [Email]

[Date]

[Personal Representative Name]
RE: Final note — [Property Address]

Dear [First Name],

This will be my final letter regarding the property at [Property Address]. I've reached out a few times over the past few months, and I don't want to be a nuisance — so I'll keep this brief.

If you've already made decisions about the property, I wish you all the best. If you're still weighing options, my offer stands:

- Cash purchase, no contingencies
- Close in as little as 10-14 days
- No agent commissions, no closing costs on your side

Please reach out at [Phone] or [Email] if there's anything I can do to help. After this, I won't contact you again unless you reach out first.

I wish your family peace and clarity as you work through this process.

Respectfully,

[Your Signature]
[Your Name]

---

## Usage Notes

- Always verify the personal representative's name from the probate petition before sending.
- Never reference the amount of equity or estimated value in letters — this can appear predatory.
- For A-grade leads, consider upgrading Touch 1 to a small notecard with a handwritten note on top of this letter.
- Check state-specific regulations on contacting estate representatives — some states restrict solicitation during probate.
