# Deal Decision Framework

Making consistent, emotion-free investment decisions requires a pre-defined framework. This guide covers minimum profit thresholds, go/no-go criteria, negotiation tactics, and deal structure options.

## Minimum Profit Thresholds by Strategy

Do not pursue deals that fall below these thresholds. They exist because unexpected costs, market shifts, and holding time overruns are statistical certainties across a deal pipeline.

**Fix-and-Flip:**
- Minimum net profit: $25,000 or 10% of ARV (whichever is higher)
- Minimum profit margin: 10% of ARV
- Minimum annualized cash-on-cash return: 30%
- Deals below these thresholds are not worth the risk and execution cost

**Wholesale:**
- Minimum assignment fee: $10,000
- Preferred assignment fee: $15,000-$25,000
- Minimum spread (MAO - acquisition price): $15,000 to leave room for buyer margin and your fee

**Buy-and-Hold:**
- Minimum cash flow: $200/month net after all expenses
- Minimum gross rent multiplier (GRM): purchase price / annual gross rent < 10 in hot markets, < 8 in secondary markets
- Minimum cap rate: 7% in secondary/tertiary markets; 5% acceptable in primary markets with high appreciation potential

## The Go/No-Go Decision Matrix

Use this framework to make a final call on any deal.

**GO if:**
- Profit meets or exceeds minimum threshold for your strategy
- ARV is confirmed by 4+ comps (confidence grade B or higher)
- Repair cost is confirmed by at least one contractor walkthrough
- You have clear path to capital (cash, hard money commitment, or JV partner confirmed)
- Exit strategy is defined and realistic for current market conditions

**NO-GO if:**
- Profit does not meet minimum threshold at the asking price
- ARV is based on fewer than 3 comps or grade C/D confidence
- Repair cost is estimated without a property walkthrough
- Any major red item (foundation, mold, environmental) is unquantified
- You cannot close within the contract timeframe

**NEGOTIATE if:**
- Deal is marginal (profit is 5-10% below your threshold)
- There is a demonstrable gap between asking price and MAO
- You can identify a specific reason the seller might accept less (motivation, time pressure, condition awareness)

## When to Negotiate vs. Walk Away

**Negotiate when:** The math shows a path to a profitable deal at a price below asking. Calculate the exact price at which the deal works and offer that number with a rationale.

**Negotiate tactics:**
- Lead with the ARV and let the math speak — share your comp analysis if you have built trust
- Offer terms the seller values: fast close, cash, no inspection contingency (only waive inspection if you have walked the property yourself)
- Be specific: "I can close at $[X] on [date] for cash, no contingencies" is more compelling than "I can pay less"
- Use silence — after presenting your number, do not speak first

**Walk away when:**
- Seller will not move within 10% of your MAO after one round of negotiation
- New information surfaces during inspection that changes repair cost significantly
- Title issues emerge that create timeline or legal uncertainty
- The property's ARV was supported by comps that have since been invalidated

## Deal Structure Options

When a deal does not pencil at standard terms, consider alternative structures before walking away:

**Subject-To:** You take over the seller's existing mortgage payments without formally assuming the loan. The seller's name stays on the loan. Useful for sellers with equity who need relief from payments. Carries risk if the lender calls the loan due on sale.

**Owner Financing (Seller Carry):** Seller acts as the lender. You make monthly payments to the seller instead of a bank. Useful when you need to minimize upfront cash or when bank financing is unavailable. Negotiate interest rate, term, and balloon payment.

**Lease Option:** You lease the property with the option to purchase at a fixed price within a defined window. Useful for properties that do not pencil as flips but have long-term appreciation potential.

**Joint Venture (JV):** Partner with another investor who provides capital in exchange for a profit split. Common structure: capital partner provides 100% of funds, active partner manages the deal, profits split 50/50 after capital return. Always document JV terms in a written agreement before closing.

**Hybrid Wholesale / Flip:** Acquire the property and complete light cosmetic work to increase the assignment fee or retail sale price. Useful when the gap between distressed value and cosmetically renovated value is $20,000+ and the work takes less than 30 days.

## Documenting the Decision

Every deal decision — go, no-go, or negotiate — should be documented in writing. Record:
- The date of the decision
- The final numbers used (ARV, repair cost, MAO, asking price)
- The source of the ARV and repair cost
- The specific reason for the decision
- Any lessons learned if the deal fell through or performed differently than projected

This documentation builds a feedback loop that sharpens your underwriting accuracy over time.
