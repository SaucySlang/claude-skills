# Repair Cost Database

Repair cost estimation is one of the highest-stakes skills in real estate investing. Underestimating repairs is the single most common cause of deal losses. This guide provides standard unit costs, regional adjustment factors, scope-of-work best practices, and contingency guidelines.

## Getting Contractor Estimates

**The three-bid rule:** Always get a minimum of three independent contractor bids for major work items. The lowest bid is rarely the right choice — look for the middle bid from a contractor with verifiable references.

**Walk the property in person:** Never estimate repairs from photos only. Walk each room and assess every major system: roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, foundation, windows, and cosmetic condition.

**Use a written scope of work:** Give every contractor the same written scope of work document and require itemized line-item bids. This makes bids comparable and creates accountability once work begins.

**Flag these red items immediately during inspection:**
- Roof age > 15 years with visible wear
- Visible foundation cracks wider than 1/4"
- Aluminum wiring (pre-1975 homes)
- Polybutylene or galvanized plumbing
- Evidence of mold or water intrusion in walls or ceilings
- Knob-and-tube electrical

Any of these could double or triple repair costs if not scoped properly at the outset.

## Scope-of-Work Best Practices

A scope of work (SOW) document should include:
1. Line item description of every task
2. Materials specification (brand, grade, or equivalent)
3. Who supplies materials (contractor or investor)
4. Start date, milestone dates, completion date
5. Payment schedule tied to milestones (never pay 100% upfront)
6. Warranty terms for labor (1 year minimum)

**Payment terms best practice:** 10% upfront for materials, 40% at rough-in completion, 40% at substantial completion, 10% holdback at final walkthrough and punch list sign-off.

## Contingency Guidelines

Always add a contingency to repair estimates. Unexpected issues are not the exception — they are the rule.

| Experience Level | Contingency |
|-----------------|-------------|
| First-time investor | 20-25% |
| 1-5 deals | 15-20% |
| 5+ deals with established contractors | 10-15% |
| Known distressed property (older home) | 20%+ regardless of experience |

For properties built before 1980, assume at minimum: asbestos testing ($300-600), possible lead paint abatement ($2,000-8,000), and aging electrical systems. Budget for these even if they may not be needed.
