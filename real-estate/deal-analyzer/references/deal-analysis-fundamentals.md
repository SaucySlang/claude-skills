# Deal Analysis Fundamentals

Understanding the financial mechanics of a real estate investment deal is the difference between building wealth and losing money. This guide covers the core formulas, key metrics, and strategic distinctions every investor needs before analyzing their first deal.

## The 70% Rule

The 70% rule is the foundational heuristic for fix-and-flip investing:

**MAO = (ARV × 0.70) - Estimated Repair Cost**

Where MAO is Maximum Allowable Offer — the most you can pay for the property and still expect an acceptable profit after all costs.

The 0.70 factor leaves 30% of ARV to cover:
- Holding costs during renovation and re-listing
- Selling costs (agent commission, closing costs)
- Your target profit margin (typically 10-15% of ARV minimum)

**When to adjust the multiplier:**
- High cost-of-living markets (California, NYC): use 0.65 or lower — costs eat more margin
- Low-cost markets with fast-flipping averages under 90 days: 0.72-0.75 may be viable
- Luxury properties (ARV > $800K): use 0.65 — holding costs and days-on-market risk are higher
- Wholesale-only (not flipping yourself): factor in your buyer's 70% rule; your acquisition must leave room for their margin plus your assignment fee

Never use the 70% rule as your only analysis. It is a quick screen, not a substitute for a full underwrite.

## Wholesale vs. Fix-and-Flip

**Wholesaling:** You contract to buy a property at a distressed price, then assign the contract to a cash buyer for an assignment fee before closing. You never own the property. Your profit is the spread between your contracted purchase price and what the end buyer pays.

Typical wholesale profit targets: $10,000-$25,000 per deal. Lower capital requirements and risk, but also lower upside. Requires a strong buyers list and fast contract execution.

**Fix-and-Flip:** You purchase the property, renovate it, and resell at retail (ARV). Higher capital requirements and risk, but 3-10× the profit of wholesale on the same deal. Requires contractor relationships, project management skills, and adequate capital reserves.

## Key Metrics

**Net Profit** = ARV - Total All-In Cost
The bottom-line dollar profit. For fix-and-flip, target a minimum of $25,000 or 10% of ARV, whichever is higher. Below $20,000, unexpected costs can easily wipe the deal.

**Profit Margin** = Net Profit / ARV × 100
Expresses profit as a percentage of sale price. Target 10-15%+ for residential flip. This metric normalizes profit across different price points, making it useful for comparing deals.

**Cash-on-Cash Return (CoC)** = Net Profit / Total Cash Invested × 100
How much you earn on the money you actually put in. Relevant when using leverage. A 20% CoC on a 6-month deal = 40% annualized, which is excellent.

**Annualized CoC Return** = (CoC Return / Deal Months) × 12
Converts deal return to an annual rate for apples-to-apples comparison with other investment types. This lets you compare a flip to stock market returns, rental yield, or other deal structures.

**Equity Multiple** = (Net Profit + Cash Invested) / Cash Invested
How many times you got your money back. A 1.3× equity multiple means you received $1.30 for every $1.00 invested. Target 1.25× or higher for a 5-6 month flip.

## Total All-In Cost Components

Never omit any of these:
1. **Purchase price** — what you pay the seller
2. **Repair cost** — with contingency
3. **Purchase closing costs** — title, escrow, recording fees (typically 2-3% for cash/hard money)
4. **Holding costs** — mortgage/interest, property taxes (prorated), insurance, utilities (monthly × holding months)
5. **Selling closing costs** — title, escrow, recording on the back end (typically 1-2%)
6. **Agent commission** — if using MLS (typically 3% buyer's agent; 6% full service)

Omitting any one of these is a common beginner mistake. Holding costs alone on a 6-month flip in a mid-price market frequently total $6,000-$12,000.
