# Contingency Negotiation Guide

Which contingencies to include or exclude, how to negotiate inspection periods, and due diligence best practices for off-market real estate acquisitions.

---

## Common Contingencies and When to Include Them

### Inspection Contingency
**Include when:** Any deal where property condition is uncertain or unverified.
**Duration:** 7–14 days depending on market competitiveness and property complexity.
**Negotiate:** Offer a shorter period (7 days) to compete with other buyers. Use the time to bring in a licensed inspector and a contractor for a repair estimate.
**Waive when:** You have already done a thorough walkthrough and have contractor bids in hand. Only waive if you have full confidence in your repair number.

### Financing Contingency
**Include when:** The deal is structured with a third-party lender (hard money, conventional).
**Duration:** 21–30 days (time needed for lender approval and appraisal).
**Note:** In off-market deals, many investors use cash or hard money and waive this contingency to compete. If you are wholesaling, you rarely need a financing contingency.
**Waive when:** You have cash or a committed hard money lender with proof of funds in hand.

### Title Contingency
**Include always.** Never waive the title contingency. Title issues (liens, encumbrances, clouded ownership) are common in distressed properties and can kill a deal at closing or expose you to liability.
**Negotiate:** This is non-negotiable from buyer's perspective. Any seller who pushes back on a title contingency is a red flag.

### Partner/Principal Approval
**Include when:** You are presenting deals to a partner, LLC, or fund that must approve before proceeding.
**Language:** "Subject to approval of buyer's business partner within [X] days." This gives you a clean exit without forfeiting EMD if the deal doesn't get internal approval.
**Duration:** 3–5 days. Longer periods may alarm sellers — frame it as a quick formality.

### Appraisal Contingency
**Include when:** Using conventional or FHA financing.
**Waive when:** Cash purchase or using hard money (hard money lenders order their own BPO/appraisal but it is not a buyer contingency).

## Inspection Period Negotiation Tactics

**Short inspection, high confidence:** Offer a 7-day inspection with a larger EMD. The larger deposit signals commitment; the short period signals you have done your homework.

**Extend by mutual agreement:** Always include language that the inspection period can be extended by [X] days with mutual written consent. Use this as a fallback if your contractor is unavailable.

**Back-to-back inspections:** On complex properties, schedule the general inspector on Day 1 and the specialty inspectors (roof, HVAC, foundation) on Days 2–3. Have contractor bids by Day 5.

**Renegotiation during inspection:** If inspection reveals material defects beyond your repair estimate, use the contingency period to renegotiate price rather than cancel outright. Present a revised offer with the inspection report attached.

## Due Diligence Best Practices

**Title search:** Order a preliminary title report from your title company immediately after going under contract. Review Schedule B exceptions carefully — mechanic's liens, HOA liens, and IRS tax liens survive closing if not cleared.

**Property taxes:** Verify current status on the county assessor website. Delinquent taxes become your responsibility at closing if not addressed in the contract.

**HOA:** Confirm HOA status, dues, and any outstanding violations or special assessments. Request the HOA's financials if purchasing in a condo or planned community.

**Survey:** Not required on every deal, but recommended if the property has boundary disputes, easements, or unusual lot configuration.

**Utilities:** Verify all utilities are in working order (or budget for reconnection if shut off). Some distressed properties have unpaid utility bills that the municipality can lien.

---

## Removing Contingencies

Remove contingencies in writing, promptly when satisfied. A verbal agreement to waive a contingency is not enforceable. Use an amendment or addendum signed by both parties. Removing contingencies moves EMD from refundable to at-risk — do not remove until you are committed to close.
