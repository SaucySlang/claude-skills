# Offer Strategy Guide

How to choose the right offer structure for each seller situation — cash, creative financing, and hybrid approaches explained.

---

## The Core Decision Framework

Every offer structure serves a different seller need. Before selecting a strategy, identify what the seller values most:

| Seller Priority | Best Offer Structure |
|---|---|
| Speed and certainty | All cash, fast close |
| Highest total price | Seller financing |
| Monthly income | Seller financing or seller carryback |
| Debt relief (over-leveraged) | Subject-to or short sale |
| Flexibility on timing | Seller finance or delayed close |
| Emotional exit (estate/probate) | Cash with seller-controlled closing date |

---

## All Cash Offers

**When to use:**
- Seller needs a quick, clean close
- Probate or divorce situations requiring certainty
- Property has title issues (cash buyers tolerate more risk)
- Seller has substantial equity and understands fair market value

**Structure:**
- Price: 65–75% of ARV minus repairs (70% rule as baseline)
- EMD: 1–2% of purchase price
- Inspection: 7–10 days
- Close: 14–21 days

**Presenting the discount:** Never apologize for the price. Frame it as the cost of certainty, speed, and convenience. Compare your net to the seller's net after agent commissions (5–6%), repairs, carrying costs, and time on market.

---

## Seller Financing

**When to use:**
- Seller owns the property free and clear (no underlying mortgage to navigate)
- Seller wants income rather than a lump sum (retirees, passive income seekers)
- Seller can't get their asking price in cash but needs to move the property
- Seller is open to an alternative if you educate them on the concept

**Structure:**
- Price: At or near asking price (the seller finance premium justifies the higher price)
- Down payment: 10–20% at close
- Rate: 4–8% (negotiate based on seller's expectations)
- Term: 60–180 months
- Security: Promissory note + deed of trust recorded against the property

**Key benefit to seller:** Total collected over the term often exceeds what they would net from a retail sale after commissions, taxes, and time. Model this out with the `offer_terms_calculator.py` tool.

## Subject-To Existing Financing

**When to use:**
- Seller is behind on payments or facing foreclosure
- Seller has a low-interest rate worth preserving (assume their 3–4% rate)
- Seller has little or no equity — subject-to relieves the mortgage obligation
- Seller is motivated primarily to escape the payment

**Structure:**
- Buyer takes title; existing mortgage stays in seller's name
- Buyer makes payments on the existing mortgage
- Seller may receive cash at close (if any equity exists) plus a monthly spread
- Due-on-sale clause: The lender has the right to call the loan due. In practice, lenders rarely exercise this when payments are current. Always disclose this risk to the seller in writing.

**Legal note:** Subject-to transactions are legal in all 50 states. Use a deed from the seller, record it, and service the existing mortgage on time. Work with a real estate attorney experienced in creative financing for proper documentation.

---

## Hybrid (Cash Down + Short Carry)

**When to use:**
- Seller wants more cash at close than pure seller finance offers
- Seller is willing to carry for a short period (2–5 years)
- Deal doesn't pencil on cash alone but seller needs some liquidity now

**Structure:**
- 20–30% cash at close
- Seller carries 70–80% of the purchase price
- Short term (24–60 months), often with a balloon payment
- Rate: market rate or slightly below to compensate for higher down

## Seller Motivation Matching

| Motivation | Recommended Structure | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Probate | Cash fast close | Long due diligence |
| Pre-foreclosure | Subject-to | Seller finance (time) |
| Tired landlord | Seller finance | Low cash offer alone |
| Divorce | Cash or hybrid | Complex creative structures |
| Relocation | Fast close cash | Delayed close |
| Retirement | Seller finance | Low total price |
| Tax reasons | Installment sale (seller finance) | Lump sum cash |
