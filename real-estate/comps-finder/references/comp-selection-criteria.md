# Comp Selection Criteria

A valid comparable sale ("comp") must meet strict standards before it can be used to estimate ARV. Using poor comps is the leading cause of overpaying for investment properties. This guide defines what qualifies as a valid comp and what disqualifies one.

## Core Validity Rules

**1. Property Type Match**
Comps must be the same property type as the subject: single-family residential with single-family, condo with condo, townhouse with townhouse. Do not mix types. A townhouse sale does not reflect single-family market dynamics, even if it's next door.

**2. Proximity (Distance)**
- Urban and suburban markets: 0.5 miles maximum from the subject property
- Rural markets: up to 1.0 mile is acceptable when closer sales are unavailable
- Never cross major barriers: highways, rivers, railroad tracks, or school district boundaries can create significant value differences even within a short distance

**3. Sold Date Range**
- Preferred: sold within 6 months of your analysis date
- Maximum: 12 months — beyond this, the sale does not reliably reflect current market conditions
- Active listings and pending sales are never valid comps — they are aspirational, not market-confirmed values

**4. Size Tolerance (Square Footage)**
- Acceptable range: ±20% of the subject property's sqft
- Preferred range: ±10% for the tightest comps
- A 1,500 sqft subject should use comps between 1,200 and 1,800 sqft
- Large size differences require large adjustments, which reduces comp reliability

**5. Arm's Length Transactions Only**
Only include sales where both buyer and seller acted in their own best interest with no special relationship or duress. Exclude:
- Foreclosure auction sales (typically below market)
- REO (bank-owned) sales (often discounted for speed)
- Short sales (seller under duress)
- Family transfers or gifts
- Sales between related corporations

**6. Same Neighborhood / Subdivision**
Comps should ideally be in the same subdivision or neighborhood as the subject. When this is impossible, confirm the competing neighborhood has similar lot sizes, school ratings, and amenity access. A comp three blocks away in a different school district may need to be discarded.

## Comp Count Requirements

| Count | Confidence Level | Action |
|-------|-----------------|--------|
| 6+ | High | Use all; discard extreme outliers |
| 4-5 | Good | Acceptable for standard underwriting |
| 3 | Marginal | Use with caution; state limitations clearly |
| 1-2 | Low | Do not make offers; gather more data first |

## When to Discard a Comp

Discard a comp even if it meets the criteria above when:
- Total adjustments required exceed $25,000 (too different from subject)
- The sale was later challenged as non-arm's-length
- The comp is in a clearly superior or inferior micro-location (corner lot premium, busy road discount)
- You cannot verify the sale details from a reliable source (county records, licensed MLS access)

## Data Sources (Ranked by Reliability)

1. County assessor / recorder records (public record, gold standard)
2. Licensed MLS access via agent or subscription
3. Redfin or Zillow sold data (generally reliable; verify against county records for disputed values)
4. Public records aggregators (useful but can lag by 30-60 days)

Always cross-reference at least two sources when the comp is pivotal to your ARV.
