# Comp Report Standards

A comp report communicates your ARV analysis to a specific audience. The format and depth should match who is reading it. This guide covers what goes into a comp report and how to tailor it for sellers, lenders, and partners.

## What Every Comp Report Must Include

Regardless of audience, every comp report needs these five elements:

1. **Subject Property Profile** — address, sqft, bed/bath, garage, pool, year built, current condition. This establishes the baseline that all adjustments reference.

2. **Comp Table** — a sortable table of all comps used: address, sold date, sale price, sqft, beds/baths, garage, pool, distance from subject, and $/sqft. This provides raw data transparency.

3. **Adjustment Grid** — shows each feature difference and the dollar adjustment applied per comp. This is the analytical heart of the report. Never omit this for lender presentations.

4. **ARV Conclusion** — states ARV low/mid/high with a clear statement of which figure is used for underwriting and why. The conclusion should be a single, unambiguous sentence.

5. **Analyst Notes** — any qualifications, unusual market factors, or data limitations. This is your professional disclosure.

## Presenting Comps to Sellers

Sellers respond to visual simplicity and narrative. Lead with the ARV conclusion. Then show the comp table. Most sellers do not want to see the adjustment grid — summarize the net adjustment per comp in a single column instead.

Key points for seller presentations:
- State the ARV as a range, not a single number. This builds credibility.
- Briefly explain what "fully renovated" means for their property — sellers often don't know what condition assumption you're making.
- Show the $/sqft comparison between their current condition and the updated comps. This makes the repair investment feel tangible.
- Avoid industry jargon (MAO, ARV, cap rate). Use plain language: "comparable sales," "market value after renovation."

## Presenting Comps to Lenders (Hard Money / Private Money)

Lenders want defensibility above all else. Lead with the adjustment grid. Show your methodology clearly. Lenders know that creative ARV inflation is common, so your transparency is a competitive advantage.

Key points for lender presentations:
- Include the data source for each comp (county assessor, MLS ID, etc.)
- State the confidence score and any limitations explicitly
- If any comp required adjustments greater than $15,000, add a footnote explaining why you included it
- Hard money lenders typically lend at 65-75% of ARV; showing your ARV conservatively helps you negotiate better terms
- Attach the comp report as a PDF with the loan application — never just quote a number

## Presenting Comps to Buyers / JV Partners

Wholesale buyers and joint venture partners want to verify your work independently. Give them the full raw data so they can pull the same comps and confirm your numbers. If your analysis holds up to independent verification, you build lasting credibility.

Key points for buyer/partner presentations:
- Include the full address of each comp (not just street name) so they can look it up
- State your adjustment methodology in one sentence so they can replicate it
- Give them the JSON data file alongside the formatted report if they request it
- Acknowledge the ARV range rather than presenting a single number — sophisticated buyers distrust single-point ARV estimates

## Formatting Standards

- Use a consistent table format throughout — do not mix narrative paragraphs with embedded numbers
- Dollar amounts: use comma separators, round to nearest $1,000 for ARV conclusions, show exact amounts in the comp table
- Dates: YYYY-MM-DD format in tables, written out (January 15, 2026) in narrative
- File naming: `comp-report-[address-abbreviated]-[YYYYMMDD].pdf`
- Page limit: 2 pages maximum for seller presentations; no page limit for lender packages

## What Not to Include

- Pending sales or active listings as comps (not market-confirmed)
- Distressed sales without disclosure that they were non-arm's-length
- Your purchase price or MAO calculation (this is internal — do not share with sellers)
- Speculative future value projections ("after the development is complete, values could reach...")
- Comps outside your stated search criteria without a written explanation for the exception
