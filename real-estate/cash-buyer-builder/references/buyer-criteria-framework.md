# Buyer Criteria Framework

## Why Capturing Criteria Matters

The most common wholesaler mistake is blasting deals to the entire buyer list regardless of fit. This trains buyers to ignore your messages. An investor who flips luxury homes in Scottsdale does not care about a $90,000 distressed property in South Phoenix — sending irrelevant deals destroys response rates and erodes trust.

A well-structured criteria capture process means every deal you send reaches buyers who are genuinely positioned to close it. This raises response rates from 5-15% (untargeted blast) to 30-60% (targeted match).

## Core Criteria to Capture

### 1. Target Markets

Ask for specific cities, zip codes, or named neighborhoods — not broad metro areas. "Phoenix" is too vague; "Laveen, 85339, and South Mountain" is actionable. A buyer who says "I'll buy anywhere in the valley" usually means they have no focus and are less likely to close quickly on any specific deal.

Criteria specificity — how tightly defined the buyer's criteria are — is a meaningful quality signal. Specific buyers have systems; vague buyers are still exploring.

### 2. Price Range

Capture both minimum and maximum. The minimum matters: some buyers avoid sub-$100K deals because title insurance and closing costs make the economics unfavorable. The maximum tells you capacity and investment strategy.

For buy-and-hold buyers, also capture target monthly rent and cash-on-cash return threshold — these are often more decision-relevant than purchase price alone.

### 3. Rehab Level

Rehab level tolerance is the most commonly misunderstood buyer criterion. Use four consistent levels:

- **Light:** Cosmetic only. Paint, flooring, fixtures, landscaping. No structural or systems work.
- **Medium:** Kitchen and bath updates, one or more systems (HVAC, roof, electrical, plumbing).
- **Heavy:** Full gut renovation, possible structural work, multiple systems replacement.
- **Teardown:** Land value play. Buyer will demolish and build new or sell the lot.

A fix-and-flip investor with one contractor crew typically maxes out at medium rehab. Developers and larger operations can handle heavy and teardown. Matching rehab level prevents wasted calls on deals that don't fit the buyer's operational capacity.

### 4. Property Type

Single-family, small multi-family (2-4 units), larger multi-family (5+), condo, mobile home, commercial, and land all attract different buyers. Don't assume — ask explicitly. Many buy-and-hold investors avoid condos due to HOA risk. Many flip investors avoid multi-family due to tenant management complexity.

### 5. Proof of Funds

Capturing criteria without verifying buying power is a critical error. Before a buyer receives deals, require a proof of funds letter (bank statement, brokerage account, or credit facility letter) dated within 30-60 days. POF verification separates serious buyers from tire-kickers.

## Segmentation Logic

After intake, assign each buyer to a primary segment:

| Segment | Typical Criteria | Deal Types to Send |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Fix-and-flip | Light-medium rehab, 70% ARV formula, 14-21 day close | Distressed SFR, cosmetic fixers |
| Buy-and-hold | Light rehab only, positive cash flow focus, 21-30 day close | Turnkey or near-turnkey rentals |
| Developer | Heavy/teardown, large lot or infill, 30-60 day close | Teardowns, large lots, assemblage |
| Hybrid | Mix of above | Match deal-by-deal |

## Criteria Refresh Cadence

Buyer criteria change. A fix-and-flip investor who just completed 3 projects may be temporarily sidelined waiting for capital to recycle. A buy-and-hold investor who just raised a new fund may have doubled their price ceiling.

- **A-tier buyers:** Criteria check every 90 days (phone call)
- **B-tier buyers:** Criteria check every 180 days (email or text)
- **C-tier buyers:** Criteria check at re-qualification before sending any deal

Always update the buyer record after a criteria conversation. An outdated profile is worse than no profile — it produces false confidence in match accuracy.

## Interpreting Criteria Red Flags

Watch for these signals of a low-quality or low-reliability buyer:

- **No proof of funds:** Not serious or using hard money without a deal in hand (fine, but document it)
- **Criteria too broad:** "I'll buy anything under $500K in Phoenix" — means they have no focus and will be indecisive
- **No closed deals:** New investors have high fall-through rates; limit blast access until they prove execution
- **Response rate below 30%:** Not engaged; re-qualify or remove before it lowers overall list metrics
- **Average close time above 45 days:** Introduces timeline risk for sellers; prioritize faster buyers in competitive situations
