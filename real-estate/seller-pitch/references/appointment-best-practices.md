# Appointment Best Practices

## Pre-Appointment Checklist

Complete every item before leaving for the appointment.

**Research:**
- [ ] Pulled 3 recent comps within 1 mile, sold within 6 months
- [ ] Confirmed estimated ARV and repair cost
- [ ] Calculated MAO and opening offer
- [ ] Reviewed all lead notes and contact history
- [ ] Run motivation score — lead is Grade A or B before spending time on appointment

**Logistics:**
- [ ] Confirmed appointment date, time, and address with seller
- [ ] Loaded comps on tablet or printed one-pager
- [ ] Printed net sheet (retail vs. cash comparison)
- [ ] Brought purchase agreement and earnest money details
- [ ] Arrived 5 minutes early — never late, never more than 10 minutes early

**Mindset:**
- [ ] Know your walk-away number cold — do not calculate it at the table
- [ ] Reviewed likely objections and response frameworks
- [ ] Reviewed seller's primary motivation trigger and opening questions

## In-Person vs Virtual Appointments

**In-Person is always preferred for:**
- Motivated sellers with Grade A scores
- Probate, divorce, or estate sales (emotional situations)
- Properties with deferred maintenance (you need to see the condition)
- Sellers who have had multiple contacts and are still uncommitted

**Virtual (video call) is acceptable for:**
- Initial discovery call with Grade B leads before committing to an appointment
- Out-of-state sellers with inherited properties
- Follow-up calls after an initial in-person visit
- Leads in early nurture phase (Grade C)

Virtual appointments close at approximately 40% the rate of in-person appointments for motivated sellers. Always push for in-person.

## How to Present Your Offer

**The sequence that works:**

1. Walk the property first (do not skip this even if the seller wants to rush to numbers)
2. Sit down and run the discovery phase (15+ minutes of listening)
3. Frame your value proposition (speed, certainty, as-is)
4. Present comps as context — not as your justification
5. Deliver the offer as a solution to their stated problem
6. Pause and let them respond

**What not to do:**
- Never apologize for your offer
- Never use the word "lowball" yourself
- Never present the number then immediately justify it — deliver and pause
- Never negotiate against yourself (if they counter, do not drop again without getting something in return)

## When to Leave Without a Signed Deal

Leave without signing when:
- Both decision-makers are not present (never get one signature without the other)
- The seller is actively hostile or combative
- You discover a title issue, occupancy issue, or lien situation requiring due diligence
- The seller's expected price is more than 15% above your MAO with no movement
- The seller is emotionally unready (e.g., spouse recently deceased, mid-divorce)

Leaving gracefully preserves the relationship. Most unconverted appointments can be won in the follow-up if you exit professionally.

## Follow-Up Timing After Appointments

**Signed contract:** Shift to closing coordination immediately. Call the title company same day.

**Warm unconverted (engaged, no objection you couldn't handle):**
- Day 3: Text — "Hope you had a chance to review the net sheet. Any questions I can answer?"
- Day 7: Call — Check in, ask if anything has changed
- Day 21: Call or handwritten card — "Checking in — wanted you to know the offer still stands."
- Day 60: Call — Re-score motivation, adjust approach if situation has changed

**Cold unconverted (multiple unresolved objections, low engagement):**
- Monthly call or postcard — keep in nurture at minimal cost
- Re-evaluate in 90 days using motivation scorer

**Dead lead (clear rejection, price gap too large, relisting with agent):**
- Log dead reason
- Set a 6-month reminder to check if the retail listing has failed
- Many dead leads come back — never burn the bridge
