# Objection Handling Playbook

## The FEEL-FELT-FOUND Framework

Every objection should be handled with a three-step response:

1. **FEEL** — Validate their current emotional state. "I feel the same way when I first see an offer."
2. **FELT** — Normalize the objection by referencing others. "Many sellers felt that way too."
3. **FOUND** — Redirect to a better outcome. "What they found is that after fees and repairs, the net was similar — but the stress was far less."

This framework works because it acknowledges the emotion before challenging the logic. Sellers need to feel heard before they can hear you.

## The 10 Most Common Seller Objections

### 1. "Your price is too low."
The most common objection. Never defend your number — instead, shift to net proceeds.

Response: "That's a fair point — let's look at what you'd actually net in both scenarios." Pull out a net sheet showing retail sale costs (6% commission, closing costs, repair budget, carrying costs for 90 days) vs. your offer.

### 2. "I need to think about it."
Usually masks a hidden concern. Isolate it.

Response: "Of course — what specifically would you like to think through?" If they cannot name a specific concern, the deal is very much alive.

### 3. "I'm already listed with an agent."
Respect the relationship. Plant a backup seed.

Response: "I completely respect that — when does the listing expire? I'd love to stay in touch as a backup in case it doesn't sell."

### 4. "I have other offers."
Compete on certainty and terms, not just price.

Response: "That's great — are those cash with no contingencies? Because a higher offer with financing contingencies can fall through. My offer is guaranteed to close."

### 5. "I want to do repairs first."
Show the math on repair costs vs. net gain.

Response: "I've seen that play out many times — let's run the numbers. If repairs take 3 months and $20k, will the sale price increase by more than $20k plus carrying costs?"

### 6. "I'm not in a hurry."
Do not push. Discover what would change their urgency.

Response: "That makes sense — what would need to happen for it to feel more time-sensitive? Is there a date or event that's driving things?"

### 7. "My spouse isn't on board."
Never proceed without both decision-makers.

Response: "I completely understand — can we get everyone together? I want to make sure all questions get answered."

### 8. "I have emotional attachment."
Lead entirely with empathy before any business discussion.

Response: "Tell me about your favorite memory in this home." Let them talk. Do not rush to numbers.

### 9. "Another investor offered more."
Verify the offer is real and apples-to-apples.

Response: "That's worth exploring — is their offer in writing? And is it as-is with no contingencies?"

### 10. "I'll just list it myself."
Acknowledge the option and introduce risk.

Response: "You absolutely can — FSBO is a real option. What's your plan if it doesn't sell in 60 days?"

## When to Push vs When to Back Off

**Push** (lean in with another question or reframe) when:
- The seller is engaged and asking questions back
- The objection is factual and addressable (price, timeline, process)
- You have not presented your full case yet

**Back off** when:
- The seller has said no three times to the same point
- The emotional objection is unresolved (grief, spousal disagreement)
- They need external approval (attorney, family member, accountant)

Backing off is not losing — it is the fastest path to a signed contract in 7-21 days instead of zero.

## Closing Techniques

**The Assumptive Close:**
"Let me pull up the purchase agreement — I'll walk you through the key terms."

**The Date Close:**
"If we do this today, I can have you at the closing table by [specific date they mentioned]. Does that work?"

**The Summary Close:**
"So to summarize: as-is sale, $[price], closing on [date], no repairs, no commissions. Does that solve the problem you came to us with?"

**The Walk-Away Close (use sparingly):**
"I want to make this work, but I'm not able to go higher than $[MAO]. I respect your position — if things change, please reach out."
