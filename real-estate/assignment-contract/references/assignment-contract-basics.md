# Assignment Contract Basics

## What an Assignment Is

An assignment of contract is the transfer of your equitable interest in a purchase agreement to a third party (the end buyer). When you put a property under contract with a seller, you hold the right to purchase that property at the agreed price. An assignment transfers that right to your buyer, who steps into your position and closes directly with the seller.

You never take title to the property. You are selling your contractual position, not the real estate itself. Your profit — the assignment fee — is the difference between what the end buyer pays for your contract rights and what you contracted to buy the property for.

## How It Works Step by Step

1. You sign a purchase agreement with the seller at $X (e.g., $160,000).
2. You find an end buyer willing to pay $X + fee (e.g., $185,000 total).
3. You execute an Assignment of Purchase and Sale Agreement with the end buyer.
4. The end buyer deposits an assignment fee (often a partial upfront, remainder at close).
5. Both parties close at the title company — the seller receives $160,000, you receive your assignment fee, the end buyer takes title.

## When an Assignment Is Legal

Assignments are legal in nearly all states as long as the original purchase contract allows it. The standard clause is "Buyer: [Your Name] and/or assigns." This language gives you the right to assign without needing additional seller consent.

An assignment is NOT permitted when:
- The purchase contract contains an explicit anti-assignment clause
- The seller added language prohibiting assignment
- You are working with an MLS-listed property where the listing agent's contract restricts assignment

If the contract is not assignable, you have two options: negotiate with the seller to add assignment language, or use a double-close instead.

## Double-Close vs Assignment

An assignment is simpler and cheaper — you never fund the purchase yourself. A double-close requires you to temporarily take title (or use transactional funding), which adds cost and complexity.

Use assignment when:
- The contract is clearly assignable
- You do not mind the buyer knowing your purchase price
- Your fee is a comfortable spread (buyers accept 5-15% of ARV as a fee)

Use a double-close when:
- The contract prohibits assignment
- Your fee is very large (>$30k) and you want to keep it private
- The seller is uncomfortable with an assignment
- The transaction involves an REO (bank-owned) property

## Title Company Requirements

Not all title companies handle wholesale assignments. Before opening title, confirm the company:
- Is familiar with assignment transactions
- Will handle simultaneous closings if needed
- Does not require both buyer and seller to be present at the same time
- Will correctly document the assignment fee disbursement

Develop a relationship with 2-3 investor-friendly title companies in your market. They understand the process, move faster, and do not ask questions that slow down your deals.

## Disclosure Obligations

You are not required to disclose your profit margin to either party unless a law specifically requires it in your state. However, transparency is often the best policy — especially with sellers who have attorneys. In some states, acting as an unlicensed broker (negotiating a deal for profit) can create legal exposure. Consult a real estate attorney in your state to confirm your specific obligations. Most experienced wholesalers hold a real estate license or work closely with licensed brokers to remain compliant.
