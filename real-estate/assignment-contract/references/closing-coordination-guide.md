# Closing Coordination Guide

## Building Title Company Relationships

Your title company is your silent partner on every deal. A good title relationship is worth more than any single deal. Find investor-friendly title companies by asking active wholesalers in your market who they use — not by Googling "title company near me."

What makes a title company investor-friendly:
- Familiar with assignment closings and double-closes
- Willing to handle same-day or back-to-back closings
- Has a dedicated escrow officer for investor transactions
- Does not require sellers and buyers to be in the same room
- Processes assignments without treating them as unusual
- Moves quickly — can open title and turn a preliminary report in 48 hours

Cultivate 2-3 relationships. Competition keeps your title company sharp and gives you a backup if one is slow or unavailable.

## Managing a Smooth Close

The wholesaler's job during closing coordination is to prevent surprises by staying ahead of every party on every milestone.

**Week 1 of contract (Days 1-7):**
- Open title immediately after contract signing — same day if possible
- Confirm earnest money is delivered and received
- Send assignment agreement to end buyer within 24 hours of executing the assignment
- Collect non-refundable deposit from buyer

**Week 2 (Days 8-14):**
- Confirm buyer has completed inspection (if any)
- Confirm buyer's funding is in place — hard money commitment letter or proof of funds
- Check in with title on preliminary title report — address any issues early
- Confirm closing date is still on track with all parties

**Days 5-3 Before Closing:**
- Call title company to confirm closing is on schedule
- Confirm buyer has wired or is ready to wire funds
- Confirm seller has all documents needed for closing
- Verify assignment fee disbursement instructions are on file with title

**Day Before Closing:**
- Final confirmation call with buyer, seller (if needed), and title company
- Confirm closing time and location (or remote closing instructions)

## What Can Go Wrong and How to Prevent It

**Title issues:** Liens, unpaid taxes, judgments, or cloudy title discovered after contract. Prevention: order preliminary title report in the first 3 days. Resolve issues immediately — do not wait until closing week.

**Buyer goes cold:** Buyer stops responding after signing the assignment. Prevention: collect a meaningful non-refundable deposit. A buyer with $3,000 on the line returns calls. A buyer with nothing at risk disappears at the first sign of trouble.

**Buyer's financing falls through:** Hard money lender changes terms or pulls approval. Prevention: always verify proof of funds or lender commitment before Day 7. Have a backup buyer in your network for your best deals.

**Seller gets cold feet:** Motivated sellers occasionally try to back out, especially if they receive a higher offer after signing. Prevention: document everything, ensure contract is fully executed, and move quickly. The faster you close, the less time a seller has to change their mind.

**Closing date missed:** Either party fails to close on the agreed date. Prevention: build milestone tracking with SLA dates using the milestone tracker. Flag overdue items immediately — do not let a missed milestone sit without action.

## Documentation Checklist

Keep copies of all documents on every deal:

- [ ] Original purchase and sale agreement (fully executed)
- [ ] Assignment of purchase and sale agreement (fully executed)
- [ ] Non-refundable deposit receipt or wire confirmation
- [ ] Preliminary title report
- [ ] Proof of funds or financing commitment (buyer)
- [ ] Earnest money receipt
- [ ] All addenda and amendments to original contract
- [ ] Closing disclosure (HUD-1 or settlement statement)
- [ ] Wire confirmation for assignment fee disbursement
- [ ] Recorded deed (obtained from title after closing)

Store all documents in a deal folder organized by deal ID. You may need these years later for tax purposes or if a dispute arises.
